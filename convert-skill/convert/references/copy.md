# The words

## The one promise

One sentence: the outcome the buyer gets, for whom, in the buyer's words. Not a feature list, not a
category ("AI-powered health platform"). Test: could a competitor put it on their page unchanged?
Then it's not yours yet. Make it specific with what only this product does (from `facts.md`).

Find it where the research overlaps: the pain buyers repeat most (voice.md, low reviews) × the
outcome they praise most (high reviews) × what the competitors don't say (competitors.md, patterns).

## The headline

- From the buyer's words. Take the phrase from voice.md's top 5; keep their nouns and verbs.
- 4–10 words. Says the outcome or names the pain. Readable by someone who has never heard of you.
- No puns, no "Unlock / Supercharge / Elevate / Revolutionize / Seamless / Next-gen / All-in-one
  platform", no "Meet X", no question headlines, no em-dash pairs.
- If the old headline is already good (the buyer's words, specific), keep it and say why.

## The sub

One or two lines: how it does it, in concrete nouns (what goes in, what comes out). Name the things
the buyer already owns or uses (their device, their tool, their file) when that's the hook.

## The first screen

Headline, sub, one primary CTA, the product (a real screenshot or photo), and one line of proof if
it exists (a real number, a real quote, real logos). Nothing else. The buyer should know what it is,
who it's for and what to press without scrolling.

## Objections

From voice.md: the questions people ask before buying and the reasons in 1–2 star reviews of the
competitors. Each objection gets answered on the page, where it comes up, in one or two lines, with
a fact from facts.md. Price, privacy, "does it work with my X", "how long to set up", "can I cancel",
"is this another subscription". The FAQ is for what's left, short (4–6 questions).

## Proof

Only real proof (facts.md): numbers counted from somewhere, quotes from real customers with their
permission, real logos. If there's none: say so plainly, show the product working instead (a real
screenshot says more than an empty claim), and put `[PROOF NEEDED: …]` where proof would go.
Competitor reviews prove the *pain* exists: they can shape a headline, never appear as testimonials.

## CTA

- Says what happens: "Get Xeyo for €5 a month", "Start free", "Book a 15-minute call". Not "Submit",
  "Learn more", "Get started" alone.
- One primary action, repeated: first screen, after the main proof, at the end. A second, quieter
  button may exist ("See how it works").
- Next to it, the line that removes the last worry: "Cancel any time", "No card needed" (only if
  true: facts.md).
- The link stays exactly the old link.

## Section order (default, change it when the research says so)

1. Hero: promise, sub, CTA, product.
2. The problem in the buyer's words (one line, maybe their quote), and the turn.
3. How it works: 3–4 steps, plain.
4. What you get: the 3–4 outcomes, each with a real product picture.
5. Proof (if real) / the honest version of it.
6. Price: the number, what's included, the cancel line.
7. FAQ: 4–6 objections left.
8. Final CTA: the promise again, the button.

Cut anything the buyer doesn't need to decide. Shorter beats complete.

## Voice

The owner's tone (from the old page), plainer. Short sentences. "You", not "users". Numbers as
digits. Keep the owner's language (an Italian site stays Italian); quotes stay in their language.
